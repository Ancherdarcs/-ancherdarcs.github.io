Found my anchor

I feel as though I have always known Jesus, but I did not truly begin to believe until I was seventeen. To understand that story, it helps to know a little of my background.

Early in my life, God provided so abundantly that I often forgot how much He was giving me, and the more I forgot, the more ungrateful I became. Looking back, I took nearly everything for granted as a child, especially my aunt, who embodied faith like no one else I have known. She came into my life when I was eight, after my mother passed away suddenly while my father was serving on active duty. I remember a stretch of time when I was so ill that I was confined to bed. I am beyond grateful for the way she nursed me back to health; without her, I am not sure I would be alive today.

Once I recovered, she decided it was time to move to the United States with her sister, and she graciously added my brother and me to the application alongside her own five children. Finding our footing here was difficult, given the many obstacles involved in establishing residency. One of the greatest challenges I faced was school. I had been a strong student in my native language, and I wanted to excel in English so I could make friends, but the more I learned, the further behind my peers I felt. That pushed me to drift between friend groups, and each one taught me how much the people around you shape who you become.

I began to rebel and became a burden to my aunt and uncle. To this day, I do not know what drove that boy, but I know it was not good. Before I can take accountability, I want to be honest about who I was. Despite their efforts to guide me, I manipulated, deceived, cheated, lied, lusted, and stole. Of all these, lust was the root of much of my behavior as a teenager, and it has shaped the direction of my life ever since. I have chased money, women, and validation, and with each pursuit, I felt my relationship with Jesus grow more distant.

When I was fourteen, I began attending summer camp, and Trout Lake Camps became one of the great catalysts of my faith. My cousins and I first went as campers in 2014 and returned for years afterward as LITs, counselors, and cooks well into adulthood. We loved that place like home; it was like a meeting point to connect with God. In 2017, sitting in the chapel, I heard "The Cure" by Unspoken. The song moved me so deeply that, in that vulnerable moment, I called the cousin I had been going to camp with. I told him I wanted to rid myself of my worst qualities, and he simply told me to anchor myself in my faith now so that God could reveal His plans for me. His words registered, though only faintly, and I carried a divided heart into adulthood, torn between the things of this world and a personal relationship with Jesus.

That struggle is why I created this page: to learn how other believers remain steadfast, and to build a space that glorifies the name of Jesus across the digital world and shares Him with those who do not yet know Him.

This forum is a welcoming place for Christians to share how God has shaped them through life's seasons, to encourage one another, and to find support in difficult times. Whether you are celebrating a breakthrough, navigating a challenge, or simply seeking prayer and fellowship, we invite you to join the conversation and discover what keeps believers anchored and steadfast through every storm.

— Omot Obang
